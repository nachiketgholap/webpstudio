About WebP Studio

A free, fast image converter that respects your privacy, made by Nachiket Gholap.

What it does

WebP Studio converts almost any common image into WebP, the modern image format developed by Google. WebP files are usually 25–35% smaller than JPEG or PNG files at similar visual quality. Smaller images make websites load faster, improve page-speed scores and use less data for everyone who visits.

Who it’s for

  • Bloggers and creators who want fast pages and images under a size limit.
  • Web developers and designers preparing assets in bulk, with folder structure kept in the ZIP.
  • Students and professionals who need to shrink images for forms, portals and email.
  • Anyone who wants to save storage without installing software or creating an account.

How it works

  • Your image is opened directly by your browser. Nothing is uploaded to any server.
  • It is decoded at full resolution and drawn onto a canvas, with high-quality step-down scaling if you choose a smaller size.
  • The result is encoded with your browser’s built-in WebP encoder. Where the browser does not have one (Safari and all iPhone and iPad browsers), the open-source libwebp encoder runs on your device instead, in a background thread.
  • With a max file size set, the tool tries several quality levels and keeps the best one that fits.
  • Transparency is kept, and photo rotation data is applied so images appear the right way up.

Privacy first

There are no accounts, cookies, analytics or ads. Your images and file names are never sent anywhere. Read the Privacy Policy for the full details.

Built for speed

The whole app is a single HTML file with no frameworks and no trackers. Images convert in a queue so the page stays responsive even with a large batch, and results update automatically when you change a setting.

Quick start

  1. On Home, drop images (or a whole folder) onto the box, tap it to choose files, or paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V (⌘+V on Mac).
  2. Leave quality on Smart, or pick another preset. Change it any time and your images re-convert automatically.
  3. Optionally choose Resize and Max file size, and turn on SEO-friendly file names.
  4. Tap Compare to check a result, then download each file, or everything at once with Download ZIP or Save separately.

Choosing a quality

Smart (default)Picks for each file: lossless for PNG, GIF, BMP, SVG and other graphics, and High for JPG, AVIF, HEIC and WebP photos. Already-compressed photos would only get bigger as lossless, so Smart avoids that.
Lossless (1.00)Logos, screenshots, diagrams and anything with text. Keeps every pixel; files are larger.
High (0.90)Photos where quality matters. Very hard to tell apart from the original.
Balanced (0.80)Websites and blogs. Big savings and still crisp.
Small (0.65)Thumbnails, previews and email, where size matters most.

You can also drag the slider to any value from 0.30 to 1.00. Each result shows the quality that was actually used.

Options

  • Resize: shrinks images so the longest side is at most the size you pick. Smaller images are never enlarged. SVGs are drawn at exactly this size, so you can export icons at any resolution.
  • Max file size: each image gets the highest quality that fits under your limit (for example, 100 KB for a blog or a form upload). If an image cannot fit even at the lowest quality, the card tells you; choose a smaller Resize size and it will fit.
  • SEO-friendly file names: turns names like “My Photo (1).PNG” into “my-photo-1.webp”: lowercase, hyphens instead of spaces, no special characters. Letters in any language are kept.
  • Folders: drop a folder onto the box, or use “Add a whole folder” on a computer. All images inside, including subfolders, are added, and the ZIP keeps the same folder structure.
  • Your settings are remembered on this device for next time.

Bulk download

  • Download ZIP: every converted image in a single ZIP file. Best for large batches and folders.
  • Save separately: saves each WebP as its own file, one after another. Your browser may ask once to allow multiple downloads; choose Allow.
  • Share (phones): sends all converted images straight to another app, such as Photos, Files or WhatsApp.
  • One at a time: use the WebP button on any card. Remove cards you don’t want before downloading in bulk.

What it can do

  • Convert PNG, JPG, GIF, BMP, AVIF, WebP, SVG and ICO, plus HEIC and TIFF in Safari.
  • Convert hundreds of images or whole folders in one go.
  • Hit a target file size automatically.
  • Compare the original and the WebP side by side with a slider, at fit or actual size.
  • Keep transparency, and turn photos the right way up using their rotation data.
  • Work on desktop, tablet and phone, including iPhone and iPad.

What it cannot do

  • Animated GIFs and animated WebPs become a single still image (the first frame).
  • Metadata such as EXIF, GPS location and colour profiles is not copied. This is good for privacy, but wide-colour photos are converted to standard sRGB colour.
  • HEIC/HEIF and TIFF open only in Safari (Mac, iPhone and iPad). In other browsers, convert them to JPG or PNG first.
  • Every browser has a size limit for images. Very large images are scaled down automatically to fit, and the card tells you when this happens. On iPhone and iPad the limit is about 16.7 megapixels.
  • SVGs are turned into pixels, so they are no longer vector graphics.
  • A ZIP can hold up to 65,535 files and 4 GB. For bigger batches, use Save separately or convert in parts.
  • Nothing is saved. Closing or reloading the tab clears your list.

Browser support

  • Chrome, Edge, Opera, Brave and Samsung Internet: use the browser’s own WebP encoder. Fastest, and Lossless gives true lossless WebP.
  • Firefox: uses its own WebP encoder (Firefox 96 or newer).
  • Safari, and every browser on iPhone and iPad: these cannot create WebP files by themselves, so WebP Studio loads a small open-source encoder (libwebp, about 280 KB) once. Results are just as good, a little slower. Your images still never leave your device.

Questions

Are my images really not uploaded?

Yes. Your images are read and converted by your own browser. No image, file name or thumbnail is sent anywhere. You can even turn off your internet after the page has loaded (on Safari and iPhone, after the first conversion) and the tool keeps working.

Why is my WebP bigger than the original?

This usually happens when an already-compressed photo (like a JPG) is saved as Lossless, or with very small graphics. Use Smart, High or Balanced, or set a Max file size. Cards that grew are marked in yellow.

My browser asks to allow multiple downloads

That happens with Save separately. Choose Allow and the rest will save. To avoid the question, use Download ZIP, which saves everything in a single download.

How do I open the ZIP file on my phone?

On iPhone, open the Files app and tap the ZIP to unpack it. On Android, open it in Files by Google (or your phone’s file manager) and tap Extract. Or skip the ZIP and use Share or Save separately.

An image couldn’t reach my max file size

Very large or detailed images may not fit a small limit even at the lowest quality. Pick a smaller Resize size (for example 1280 px) and it will usually fit easily.

A very large image failed or the page slowed down

Set Resize to 4096 px (or smaller) and tap Retry. Converting fewer huge images at a time also helps on phones.

My HEIC photo will not open

Only Safari can read HEIC. Open this page in Safari, or convert the photo to JPG first. On iPhone, photos you pick from your library are usually handed over as JPG automatically.

Where are my converted files stored?

Only in your browser’s memory, until you download them or close the tab. Nothing is kept afterwards.
